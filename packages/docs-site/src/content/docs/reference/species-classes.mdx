A **species class** is a category such as mammalian, lithoid, machine, or
presapient. It connects a species to an archetype, portraits, graphical
culture, creator rules, and a large set of descriptive words. The SDK writes
definitions under `common/species_classes/`.

`archetype` supplies the broad biological or machine behavior. `trait` supplies
the class-wide species trait. `graphicalCulture` selects ship and city visuals,
while `movePopSoundEffect` selects the population-movement sound. The SDK
checks the graphical-culture reference in the example; the other raw ids must
exist in Stellaris or another mod.

Vanilla's main playable classes often omit explicit portraits because the game
also has portrait-set data associated with those classes. For a custom class,
use `portraits`, `customPortraits`, and the exclusion lists when you need to
control the exact pool. A `customPortraits` row can apply separate
scope-independent `playable` and `randomized` gates to its portrait list.

<Aside type="note" title="A species class is not a portrait pack">
  The registry connects portrait ids and groups to class behavior. It does not create portrait GFX,
  model entities, sounds, traits, or species archetypes. Supply those through vanilla or the
  appropriate mod files.
</Aside>

## Separate creator eligibility from random generation

`playable` controls whether the class is available to the player.
`randomized` controls random use and accepts either a boolean or a trigger.
Vanilla DLC classes commonly use matching ownership triggers for both. Hidden
or presapient classes commonly use `playable: always(false)` and
`randomized: false`.

`possible` and `possibleSecondary` are government requirement blocks, not
ordinary triggers. Use them to constrain authority, ethics, origin, and civic
combinations in the species creator. Their nested `value`, `not`, `nor`, and
`orGroups` shapes are listed in the generated field table. Copy the structure
of the closest vanilla class and preserve its user-facing rejection text.

## Keep uplift and creator pools consistent

For a presapient class, `upliftedInto` identifies the resulting species class
and `upliftedPortraits` controls the resulting portrait pool. Vanilla's
`PRE_MAM`, for example, is not playable or randomized and uplifts into `MAM`.

The remaining portrait lists have narrower roles:

- `nonRandomizedPortraits` excludes portraits from random generation.
- `nonPreFtlPortraits` excludes portraits from pre-FTL species.
- `customPortraits` groups portraits behind its own playable and randomized
  conditions.

The vocabulary fields generate localization keys for class names, plurals,
insults, compliments, anatomy, fossils, and remnants. Supply the terms your
content can display, and see [Localization](/concepts/localization/) for adding
translations.

Test the species creator, random empires, prescripted countries, portrait
selection, uplift, genemodding or robomodding, ship-set selection, movement
audio, every government restriction, and save/load in Stellaris. See
[Testing your mod](/guides/testing-your-mod/).

## API and vanilla references

- [`SpeciesClassDef`](https://github.com/yeager-j/pdx-ts-sdk/blob/main/packages/sdk/src/generated/species-class.ts)
  is the generated definition used by `mod.speciesClass`.
- [`vanilla.speciesClass`](https://github.com/yeager-j/pdx-ts-sdk/blob/main/packages/sdk/src/generated/vanilla-refs.ts)
  provides checked references to vanilla species-class ids.

Species classes have no patch surface. `mod.patchSpeciesClass` does not exist.
