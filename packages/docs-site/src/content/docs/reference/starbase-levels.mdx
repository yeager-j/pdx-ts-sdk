A **starbase level** maps one progression level to a starbase ship size and its
station-level policy. It controls the next upgrade, capacity use, module and
building positions, map and outliner display, home-base and trade behavior,
AI weight, construction rules, and starbase-view art. The SDK writes levels
under `common/starbase_levels/`.

Starbase levels attach to the wider [ship stack](/concepts/ships/) at the ship
size. They do not define that hull's sections, components, or fixed design.

## A level selects a hull; it does not assemble one

`shipSize` is required. The selected size must already describe a usable
starbase hull, with its own section, component, graphics, and construction
contracts.

```ts
const relayBastion = mod.starbaseLevel("bastion", {
  shipSize: vanilla.shipSize("starbase_starhold"),
  levelWeight: 2,
  starbaseCapacityUsed: 1,
  moduleSlots: [1, 2, 3, 4],
  buildingSlots: [1, 2],
});
```

Changing `moduleSlots` does not add module hardpoints to a model, and changing
`shipSize` does not migrate the old hull's component rules. Read the complete
vanilla level and its selected ship size together.

  every upgrade and downgrade in Stellaris.
</Aside>

## Level weight, AI weight, and capacity answer different questions

- `levelWeight` is the station's progression weight. Vanilla uses it in claim
  cost and requirements for modules and buildings.
- `aiWeight` is a starbase-scoped weight block with the owning country as
  `FROM`; it ranks level behavior for the AI.
- `starbaseCapacityUsed` is the number of starbase-capacity units consumed by
  a station at this level.

Do not derive one from another. A strategically important special level can
have a high progression weight, a conditional AI weight, and a capacity cost
chosen for balance.

## Module and building slots are position lists

`moduleSlots` and `buildingSlots` list engine slot positions that the UI and
station support. Ordinary levels use integers. The generated types also admit
special positions such as `spinal_mount_1` and `lower_1` through `lower_4`.

The list values are not counts. `[1, 2, 3, 4]` exposes four named positions;
`[4]` does not mean four slots. Preserve the exact special positions required
by the selected hull and starbase UI.

Test modules and buildings in every position, including upgrades and
downgrades with occupied slots. The SDK does not simulate retention, removal,
or invalid buildings during a level transition.

## Display, trade, and home-base policy are independent

The registry separates several UI and behavior switches:

- `showInOutliner`, `displayMapIcon`, and `displayEmpireShield` control
  visibility in different surfaces.
- `displayShipyardButton` controls the station-view shipyard action.
- `potentialHomeBase` lets fleets select the starbase as a home base.
- `collectsTrade` controls whether trade collection and protection range
  modifiers affect this level.
- `canAlwaysDismantle` relaxes dismantling restrictions.

Many fields have game defaults. State a value when the level intentionally
differs or when the policy is important to the lesson; do not assume an
outliner switch also controls the map or empire shield.

## Upgrade and downgrade triggers run on the starbase

`upgradePossible` and `downgradePotential` are starbase-scoped triggers.
`aiWeight` also evaluates on the starbase and exposes the owner as `FROM`.

Use the triggers for state that belongs to the current station and its owner.
Keep technology, empire policy, system state, and special construction rules
at the same seam used by the vanilla chain you are extending.

`specialConstruction` marks a specialized construction path; it does not
provide that path. A new level still needs a source that creates or advances a
starbase to its id.

## Pictures, portraits, and localization keys

`picture` accepts either one sprite or a conditional block with a
starbase-scoped trigger and sprite. Vanilla uses the conditional form to swap
the station-view background for bioship empires. `portrait` selects separate
station art.

`levelTooltip` is an existing localization key. The registry does not create
English text for it, so declare that key through the mod's localization
channel when it belongs to this mod.

Graphics must agree with the selected ship size and starbase UI. The SDK checks
sprite reference shapes, not whether the final composition renders.

## Current limits and testing advice

- Starbase levels have no patch surface. Existing vanilla progression cannot
  be changed through `mod.patchStarbaseLevel`.
- The SDK cannot prove that a custom first level is reachable, a chain is
  acyclic, the hull supports the listed slots, or upgrades preserve a valid
  station.
- Special construction and dismantling behavior need their surrounding game
  system; the flag alone is not a complete feature.

Test initial creation, every upgrade and downgrade, AI behavior, capacity use,
claim and building requirements, occupied module and building slots, home-base
selection, trade, outliner and map display, pictures, portraits, dismantling,
destruction, ownership changes, and save/load at each level.
