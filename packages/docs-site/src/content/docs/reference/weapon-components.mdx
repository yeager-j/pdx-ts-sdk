A **weapon component template** defines one weapon that can occupy a compatible
section hardpoint. It owns the weapon's socket compatibility, how it delivers
damage, and what happens on a hit. The SDK writes weapons into
`common/component_templates/`.

The [Ships overview](/concepts/ships/) builds a complete fixed design that
mounts one custom medium beam in three named section slots.

## Three definitions must agree on compatibility

A mounted weapon crosses three layers:

1. The [section template](/reference/section-templates/)'s
   `componentSlot[].template` selects a component-slot template such as
   `medium_turret`.
2. The weapon's `size` and behavior must be compatible with that socket.
3. A [global ship design](/reference/global-ship-designs/)'s `component[].slot`
   names the section slot and its `template` names this weapon.

The weapon's [component set](/reference/component-sets/) groups it with related
sizes or upgrade tiers. The set does not select the hardpoint.

A weapon declares its socket compatibility with `size`, its family with
`componentSet`, and its weapon type with `tags`:

```ts
const beam = mod.weaponComponentTemplate("beam", {
  icon: "GFX_ship_part_laser_1",
  componentSet: beamSet,
  size: "medium",
  type: "instant",
  tags: ["weapon_type_energy", "m_slot"],
  projectileGfx: "infrared_laser_m",
  damage: { min: 10, max: 24 },
  range: 60,
  accuracy: 0.9,
  tracking: 0.3,
});
```

The SDK checks field values and references but does not prove that a component
slot accepts a particular weapon or that the slot's model locator can display
its art.

## Delivery, art, and damage are separate

`type` selects the weapon delivery mode described by the game rules.
`projectileGfx` selects the beam or projectile definition, and `entity` can
select a mounted weapon entity. Those graphics fields do not supply combat
numbers.

The combat payload groups into a damage core (`damage`, the hull, armor, and
shield multipliers, the two penetration fields, and the collateral and
size-damage factors), geometry (`range`, `minRange`, `firingArc`, `accuracy`,
`tracking`), missile durability (`missileSpeed` and its companions), and timing
(`windup`, `totalFireTime`).

Define a coherent delivery model. Missile durability does not make sense for
an instant beam, and beam art does not make missile movement values useful.

### Vanilla keeps its weapon numbers in a CSV

Vanilla defines almost every weapon number in
`common/component_templates/weapon_components.csv`, and mutation weapons in
`mutation_weapon_components.csv`, with one row per weapon key. No vanilla
weapon script block sets `damage`, `range`, `tracking`, `power`, `windup`, or
`cooldown`; every named vanilla weapon has a CSV row instead. A vanilla weapon
opened as a baseline therefore shows tags, prerequisites, `potential`, and art,
and no combat values.

The rules accept both places, so an SDK weapon writes its numbers inline as Def
fields, as the example above does. Authoring a new weapon works. Two things do
not. The SDK writes only `.txt`, so it cannot rebalance a vanilla weapon the way
vanilla itself is balanced, and two CSV-only columns, `cooldown` (rate of fire)
and `military_power_multiplier`, have no Def field at all.

A few vanilla weapons set a value in both places and the two disagree.
`SMALL_BIO_PLASMA_2` has `accuracy = 1` in the script and `0.80` in the CSV row.
The game does not document which source wins, so do not read a vanilla script
value as that weapon's effective stat.

<Aside type="caution" title="Graphics still require model locators">
  A mounted weapon entity needs the culture-aware art and muzzle locator expected by Stellaris. The
  SDK cannot inspect the entity or prove that projectiles leave from the intended hardpoint.
</Aside>

## Availability, upgrades, and designer policy share the base fields

Weapon components use the same family and selection fields as the other
component registries:

- `prerequisites`, `potential`, `possible`, `validForCountry`, and
  `showTechUnlockIf` gate availability;
- `componentSet`, `upgradesTo`, and `upgradePath` shape the component family;
- `tags` are game-mechanical, not designer hints: the `weapon_type_*` tags
  decide which weapon-type modifiers reach the weapon and which turret entity
  the component-slot template draws, and the slot-class tags (`s_slot`,
  `m_slot`, `l_slot`, `x_slot`) classify the weapon in the designer;
- `aiTags`, `aiTagWeight`, `aiWeight`, and `shouldAiUse` guide automatic
  selection.

A weapon with no `weapon_type_*` tag is left out of every weapon-type modifier
and renders no turret in a slot that picks its entity by tag, such as
`medium_turret`.

`potential` decides whether the weapon appears in the list for a slot.
`possible` lets the weapon forbid other components in the same design. Both are
design-scoped with the owning country as `FROM`. Vanilla weapons use `potential`
only.

Four older fields are deprecated and no current vanilla weapon uses them:
`sizeRestriction` and `classRestriction` are replaced by `potential`, and
`shipLimit` and `blockedBy` are replaced by `possible`. Put new work in the
trigger seams.

## Target selection can be broad or explicit

`targetType`, `targetFocus`, `targetWeights`, `targetableShipSizes`,
`useShipMainTarget`, `prioProjectile`, and `pointDefenceTargets` influence how
the weapon selects targets.

Keep weapon tags, AI tags, and target policy consistent. A point-defense
weapon needs the projectile or strike-craft target categories it is expected
to intercept. A spinal weapon needs a firing arc and role that match its
section and ship behavior.

## `onHit` runs on the target ship

`onHit` records an effect on the target ship with the firing ship as `FROM`
(see [Scope and context](/guides/scope-and-context/)). Use it for gameplay that
belongs to a successful hit, not for ordinary damage numbers already
represented by the weapon fields.

Keep the effect small and deterministic. High-rate weapons can execute it many
times, and the SDK does not simulate performance, stacking, immunities, or
multiplayer behavior.

## Costs, modifiers, and auras

`resources` supports component construction cost and upkeep. Weapon templates
also admit ship and design modifiers, repeated triggered modifiers, an
`injectedModifier`, and friendly or hostile auras. These are independent of the
core damage block and can affect the mounting ship or other ships.

`injectedModifier` pairs a modifier with its own `stackInfo`. An aura carries a
`limit` trigger for the condition an affected ship must meet, `radius` or
`systemWide` for reach, `applyOn` for whether the modifier lands on individual
ships or on whole fleets, stack information, and graphics. Only `hostileAura`
takes `damagePerDay`. Use stable stack ids and test repeated weapons on the
same ship and across a fleet.

## Current limits and testing advice

- Weapon components have no patch surface.
  `mod.patchWeaponComponentTemplate` does not exist.
- The SDK writes only `.txt`. It cannot emit `weapon_components.csv`, so
  `cooldown` and `military_power_multiplier` are out of reach.
- The game documents several weapon keys the vendored rules do not carry, so
  the Def has no field for them: `chain_damage`, `chain_range`, and
  `chain_count` for arc weapons; the min/max block form of `collateralDamage`,
  which the Def takes as a single number; `max_range`; `slot_restriction`, the
  weapon-side counterpart to the section and design controls above; and
  `build_time`. No current vanilla weapon uses any of them either.
- The game's own documentation shows `damagePerDay` inside `friendlyAura`, but
  the rules give it to `hostileAura` only, and no vanilla component uses it on
  either side.
- The SDK cannot simulate damage per day, firing cadence, target choice,
  projectile interception, on-hit frequency, aura stacking, or AI design
  quality.
- The generated type accepts many combinations because the game grammar does;
  that is not proof that every combination is meaningful.

Test slot compatibility, design availability, upgrades, power and cost,
tooltip values, firing arcs, muzzle positions, every target class, damage and
penetration, projectile behavior, on-hit effects, AI selection, combat
performance, and save/load.
