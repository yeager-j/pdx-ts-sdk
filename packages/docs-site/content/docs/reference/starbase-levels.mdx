A **starbase level** maps one progression level to a starbase ship size and its
station-level policy. It controls the next upgrade, capacity use, module and
building positions, map and outliner display, home-base and trade behavior,
AI weight, construction rules, and starbase-view art. The SDK writes levels
under `common/starbase_levels/`.

Starbase levels join the [ship registries](/concepts/ships/) at the ship size.
They do not define that hull's sections, components, or fixed design.

## A level selects a hull; it does not assemble one

`shipSize` is required. The selected size must already describe a usable
starbase hull, with its own section, component, graphics, and construction
contracts. See [Ship sizes](/reference/ship-sizes/) for the hull itself.

Changing `moduleSlots` does not add module hardpoints to a model, and changing
`shipSize` does not migrate the old hull's component rules. Read the complete
vanilla level and its selected ship size together.

Each vanilla level owns its hull one to one: across the 41 shipped levels, no
`ship_size` is used twice. The worked example below departs from that and
reuses the vanilla starport and starhold hulls to keep the chain short. Nothing
in the SDK or in the game's own documentation says what happens when two levels
claim one hull, and effects such as `create_starbase` take a hull rather than a
level id. Prefer a dedicated ship size for a chain you ship.

Nothing reaches this chain on its own. An existing level's `nextLevel`, or the

`relay_bastions_starbase_level_port`; see

outpost chain cannot be edited in place. See
[Patching vanilla](/guides/patching-vanilla/).

<Callout type="warn" title="Avoid loops and dead ends">
  The SDK checks references but does not follow `nextLevel`. A cycle compiles, and so does a chain
  that nothing points into. Walk the whole chain yourself and test it in Stellaris.
</Callout>

## Level weight, AI weight, and capacity answer different questions

- `levelWeight` is the station's progression weight. Vanilla uses it in claim
  cost and requirements for modules and buildings.
- `aiWeight` is a starbase-scoped weight block with the owning country as
  `FROM`; it ranks level behavior for the AI.
- `starbaseCapacityUsed` is the number of starbase-capacity units consumed by
  a station at this level.

Do not derive one from another. A strategically important special level can
have a high progression weight, a conditional AI weight, and a capacity cost
chosen for balance.

## Module and building slots are position lists

`moduleSlots` and `buildingSlots` list engine slot positions that the UI and
station support. Ordinary levels use integers. The generated types also allow
the special positions `spinal_mount_1` and `lower_1` through `lower_4`.

The list values are not counts. `[1, 2, 3, 4]` exposes four named positions;
`[4]` does not mean four slots. Preserve the exact special positions required
by the selected hull and starbase UI.

That special set is closed. The game treats slot keys as plain strings and
documents whole `spinal_mount_*` and `lower_*` families, but the vendored rules
list only the five names above, so `spinal_mount_2` or `lower_5` is a compile
error. Vanilla stays inside the same set.

## Display, trade, and home-base policy are independent

`showInOutliner`, `displayMapIcon`, and `displayEmpireShield` are three
separate switches over three separate surfaces. Turning off the outliner entry
does not hide the map icon or the empire shield. `showInOutliner` can also be
overruled from outside the level: the game ignores it when the starbase _type_
sets `is_waystation = yes`.

`displayShipyardButton` defaults to yes and shows the shipyard button in the
fleet UI of mobile starbases, such as arkships. It does not control a static
starbase's station view. Every vanilla use sets it to `no` on an arkship level.

`potentialHomeBase` and `collectsTrade` are independent of the display
switches and of each other. Many of these fields have game defaults, listed in
the field table below. State a value when the level intentionally differs, or
when the behavior matters for this level.

## Upgrade and downgrade triggers run on the starbase

`upgradePossible` and `downgradePotential` are starbase-scoped triggers.
`upgradePossible` decides whether a station at this level may upgrade to
`nextLevel`. `downgradePotential` decides whether it may return to the previous
level; the game blocks downgrading on mobile starbases whatever the trigger
says. The worked example sets both to `always()`, which is the permissive
case. A real chain usually restricts at least the upgrade, the way vanilla's
orbital rings require a planetary ascension tier.

Use these triggers for state that belongs to the current station and its owner.
Put technology and empire-policy gates where the vanilla chain you extend puts
them, so the level does not re-check what the game has already gated.

## Construction and dismantling flags

`canAlwaysDismantle` allows dismantling this starbase even when its system has
colonies.

`specialConstruction` is accepted by the rules, but no source says what it
marks: the game ships no documentation for it, no vanilla level sets it, and
the key appears nowhere in the game files. Treat it as unverified. A new level
still needs a source that creates or advances a starbase to its id.

## Pictures, portraits, and localization keys

`picture` accepts either one sprite or a conditional block with a
starbase-scoped trigger and sprite. Vanilla uses the conditional form to swap
the station-view background for bioship empires. `portrait` is undocumented in
the same way as `specialConstruction`: the rules accept a sprite, the game
documents no meaning for it, and no vanilla level sets it.

`levelTooltip` stores a localization key. Write the display text and the SDK
keys it against the level and the field, or pass a reference to an entry that
already exists — see [Localization](/concepts/localization/).

Graphics must agree with the selected ship size and starbase UI. The SDK checks
sprite reference shapes, not whether the final composition renders.

## Current limits and testing advice

- The three sibling registries are not authorable. The rules declare
  `starbase_module`, `starbase_building`, and `starbase_type` beside
  `starbase_level`, but the SDK exposes only levels: there is no
  `mod.starbaseModule`, `mod.starbaseBuilding`, or `mod.starbaseType`. A custom
  level can only be filled by vanilla or third-party modules and buildings, and
  the waystation behavior that overrules `showInOutliner` lives on the type.
- The SDK cannot prove that a custom first level is reachable, a chain is
  acyclic, the hull supports the listed slots, or upgrades preserve a valid
  station. It does not simulate retention, removal, or invalid buildings during
  a level transition.
- Special construction and dismantling behavior need their surrounding game
  system; the flag alone is not a complete feature.

Test in the live game:

- creation of the first level, and that something reaches it;
- every upgrade and downgrade, with modules and buildings in each occupied
  slot;
- capacity use, claim cost, and module and building level requirements;
- AI behavior across the chain;
- outliner and map display, empire shield, home-base selection, and trade;
- pictures, portraits, and level tooltip text;
- ownership change, dismantling, destruction, and save/load at each level.

See [Testing your mod](/guides/testing-your-mod/) for the boundary between SDK
checks and live-game verification.
