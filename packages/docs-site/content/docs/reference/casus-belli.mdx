A **casus belli** is a cause for war. It decides which empires can hold that
cause, when it is valid against a target, when a scripted grant expires early,
and whether it appears in diplomacy or creates a notification. The SDK writes
casus belli definitions under `common/casus_belli/`.

## Connect the cause to a war goal

A casus belli does not define the outcome of a war. A
[war goal](/reference/war-goals/) points back to it and supplies the peace,
acceptance, and end-of-war behavior. This example defines both sides of that
relationship and passes the owned casus belli directly to `casusBelli`:

the war goal in every intended diplomatic state.

## Potential, validity, and destruction are different gates

- `potential` runs on the attacker and filters the countries that may use this
  kind of cause.
- `isValid` runs daily with the attacker as `THIS` and the target as `FROM`. It
  creates or removes automatically evaluated causes, but does not govern a
  cause granted directly by script.
- `destroyIf` uses the same scopes and can remove a scripted grant before its
  normal expiration.

The example is a simplified claim/conquest-shaped pair: it admits only
ordinary, non-nomadic countries and keeps the cause only while the attacker has
a claim on the target. Vanilla's `cb_claim` adds federation, total-war,
overlord, subject, and subject-claim guards. Copy those surrounding rules when
the custom cause should behave like the vanilla cause. Vanilla's
`cb_subjugation` is a useful contrasting case. It has relative-power and crisis
checks, can expire early through `destroy_if`, and is shared by more than one
war goal.

<Callout type="warn" title="Scripted grants use a different lifetime">
  A duration on an effect that grants a casus belli and the definition's `destroyIf` can both end
  the grant. Test automatic acquisition and scripted acquisition separately.
</Callout>

## Notifications, diplomacy, and proxy wars

`showNotification` is required by the generated TypeScript contract even
though the game describes it as defaulting to yes. Set it deliberately. Vanilla
claim causes use `false` to avoid duplicate claim notifications.

`showInDiplomacy` controls whether the cause is shown on the diplomacy surface.
`proxyWarResources` and `onProxyWarStart` apply only when the cause is selected
for a proxy war: the first records the instigator's cost, and the second runs
when that proxy war is validated.

Test acquisition and removal, target changes, diplomatic visibility,
notifications, every linked war goal, proxy-war cost and effects, and save/load
in Stellaris. See [Testing your mod](/guides/testing-your-mod/).
